# Initiation
> **Gelenek:** Multiple traditions including Hermeticism, Gnosticism, Esoteric Christianity, Shamanism, Mithraic Mysteries, Dionysiac Mysteries, Martinism, Witchcraft, and various occult and esoteric systems · **Amaç:** Ontological metamorphosis, purification of being, spiritual transformation, admission into secret knowledge and brotherhoods, death and rebirth, access to hidden mysteries of cosmos and God · **Timing:** Astrologically significant moments: sun in first half of day, Mercury in Sign of Virgin having passed 15 degrees (Hermetic); specific nights chosen for occasion (witchcraft) · **Tür:** kavram
*Initiation is a ritual process of spiritual transformation through which an individual undergoes symbolic death and rebirth to gain access to esoteric knowledge, enter into secret societies or mystery traditions, and achieve altered states of consciousness enabling them to perceive and operate in multiple realms of existence simultaneously.*

Initiation represents a fundamental concept across numerous esoteric and religious traditions as an ontological metamorphosis or purification of being effected through knowledge of and participation in hidden mysteries. In Hermetic literature, particularly the Discourse on the Eighth and the Ninth Sphere (NHC VI), initiation involves elevation of the mind through planetary spheres into the eighth and ninth spheres, accompanied by mystical experience of divine Light-power, adoption into the brotherhood of Hermes Trismegistus, and study of hermetic books combined with pious life. The initiate must swear oaths by elements and deities, with the experience being ineffable and requiring prior spiritual preparation through 'general' and 'detailed' discourses. In Romantic esotericism, initiation structures literary works as quests for Castle, Sanctuary, Treasure, Secret, involving ordeals of Labyrinth, dark Forest, Ascent of Mountain or Descent into Underworld. The labyrinth specifically symbolizes initiation as reaching the center—the human heart/essence of microcosm—followed by journey beyond material existence. The Mithraic mysteries featured climbing a seven-rung holy ladder of different metals, each symbolizing planetary numerals and deities. Shamanic initiation in Central Asia, China and Black Sea regions involved climbing to celestial realms and returning. In demonological and esoteric symbolism, the dragon-man represents an initiate as composite creature (human upper, demonic lower) able to live in two realms with equal facility; the higher man born from struggle with lower nature. The mask in Oceanic cultures serves in initiation ceremonies for transition from youth to adulthood, with mud/clay masks covering faces until washing reveals 'new face' of adult. In witchcraft traditions, initiation involved formal admission ceremonies with grades and hierarchies: children

under guardianship, initiates with guardians, performers without guardians, masters as propagandists/initiators/tutors, and leaders (King/Queen). The Basque witch trials describe systematic organization with master wizard sponsoring converts, branding, secrecy oaths, indoctrination, and initiation ceremonies. Psyche's myth encodes initiation through tasks representing death and rebirth, journey to Hades, and eventual immortality. Jungian psychology presents individuation as modern equivalent of initiatic path—mystical process of integrating unconscious elements to achieve Self-realization, with alchemical stages (nigredo, albedo, rubedo) interpreted as psychic states in confrontation between consciousness and unconscious. The key symbolizes initiation across traditions, linked to Janus as god of initiation, with silver key representing psychological revelation, gold key philosophical wisdom, diamond key power to act. The door and gate symbolize initiation as passage between states—'sleeping' unconscious person entering and 'conscious' new person exiting, with gates guarded by monsters representing struggle with baser nature. Martinism developed elaborate initiation systems with Martinezist theurgical operations versus Saint-Martinist mystical approaches, involving consecration rituals for water, salt, ashes, fire, and evocation of elementals. The FUDOSI (Universal Federation of Orders and Societies of Initiation) was created 1934 to coordinate initiatory organizations. Secrecy surrounding initiation serves multiple functions: protection of mysteries from profanation, ineffability of experience, and pedagogical gradualism through veiled discourse.

### Katılımcılar
Varies by tradition: solitary individuals in Hermetic mysticism; pairs (initiator and candidate) in many systems; groups in Mithraic and witchcraft traditions; master wizards and sponsors in Basque witchcraft; analysts and patients in Jungian 'initiation' as transferential relationship

### Adımlar
Preparation through study and pious life (Hermetic); journey through seven planetary spheres (Hermetic); prayer to supreme God 'to whom one speaks in silence'; embrace between initiator and candidate; experience of divine Light-power descent; oath by elements and deities; recording experience on turquoise steles placed in sanctuary at astrologically significant moment; climbing seven-rung ladder of different metals (Mithraic); covering face with mud/clay mask then washing (Oceanic); sponsorship by master wizard, abandonment of previous faith, branding, secrecy oath, indoctrination, formal ceremony (witchcraft); stages of general then detailed discourses leading to direct vision

### Tools
Seven-rung ladder of different metals (Mithraic); turquoise steles with hieroglyphic characters (Hermetic); masks of mud or clay (Oceanic); keys (silver, gold, diamond) symbolizing degrees of knowledge; ointments for flying (witchcraft); books of hermetic teachings; astrological timing instruments
