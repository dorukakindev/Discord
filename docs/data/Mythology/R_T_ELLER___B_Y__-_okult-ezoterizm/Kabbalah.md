# Kabbalah
> **Tür:** mistisizm
*The mysticism of classical Judaism, founded on the Torah, involving secret oral tradition handed down from teacher to pupil. It is a means for achieving union with God while maintaining an active life in the mundane world. The term derives from Hebrew QBL (Qibel), meaning 'to receive' or 'that which is received.'*

![gorsel](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c3/Tree_of_Life%2C_Medieval.jpg?utm_source=tr.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=api&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)

Kabbalah is derived from the Hebrew word QBL (Qibel), meaning 'to receive' or 'that which is received,' referring especially to a secret oral tradition handed down from teacher to pupil. The term was first used in the 11th century by Ibn Gabirol, a Spanish philosopher, and has since become applied to all Jewish mystical practice. It is founded on the Torah but is not an intellectual or ascetic discipline; rather, it is a means for achieving union with God while maintaining an active life in the mundane world. According to lore, God taught what became the Kabbalah to angels. After the Fall, angels taught the knowledge to Adam to provide humans a way back to God. The knowledge was passed to Noah, then to Abraham and Moses, who initiated 70 elders. Kings David and Solomon were initiates. Influenced by Gnosticism and Neoplatonism, the oral tradition passed into the tradition and literature of the Merkabah mystics (ca. 100 B.C.E.–1000 C.E.). Merkabah means 'God's Throne-Chariot' and refers to the chariot of Ezekiel's vision. The goal of the Merkabah mystic was to enter the throne world and perceive God sitting upon his throne, reached after passing through seven heavens while in an ecstatic trance state. The passage was dangerous, impeded by hostile angels; talismans, seals, sacred names of angels, and incantations were required to navigate through obstacles. The historical origin of true Kabbalah centers on the Sefer Yetzirah (Book of Creation), attributed to Rabbi Akiba, martyred by Romans. The book's exact date is unknown; it was in use in the 10th century but may have been authored as early as the third century. It presents cosmology and cosmogony and sets forth the central structure of Kabbalah, reputed to contain the formula for creation of a golem. In 917, practical kabbalism was introduced by Aaron ben Samuel in Italy; it spread through Germany as German kabbalism

or Early Hasidim, drawing upon Merkabah practices—ecstatic, with magic rituals, prayer, contemplation, and meditation. The magical power of words and names assumed great importance, giving rise to gematria, notarikon, and temurah. The Classical Kabbalah was born in the 13th century in Provence, France, and moved to Spain where it was developed by medieval Spanish Jews. The primary work is the Sefer Zohar (Book of Splendor), attributed to second-century sage Rabbi Simeon bar Yohai but actually written 1280-1286 by Spanish kabbalist Moses de Leon. According to lore, it comprises teachings given to Rabbi Simeon by divine revelation. The teachings became known as Spanish Kabbalah and spread into Europe in the 14th and 15th centuries. After the expulsion of Jews from Spain in 1492, Kabbalah study became more public. Isaac Luria Ashkenazi (1534-72), called Ari Luria, student of Moses Cordovero (1522-70), conceived bold new theories giving Kabbalah new terminology and complex symbolism, emphasizing letter combinations as medium for meditation and mystical prayer. In the 14th century, Practical Kabbalah developed, involving magical techniques for making amulets and talismans and invoking spirits. It is complex, featuring magical alphabets, secret codes of communication with angels. The Hasidic movement emerged from Lurianic Kabbalah and made Kabbalah accessible to the masses; Hasidim are the only major branch of modern Judaism to follow mystical practices. Interest among Jews declined after the 18th century. The Reconstructionist movement, founded 1922 by Rabbi Mordecai M. Kaplan, borrows from Hasidic traditions and espouses more mystical Judaism. Interest enjoyed cross-cultural renewal beginning late 20th century as part of broad interest in esoteric subjects. Western occult interest grew first from German kabbalism and Lurianic kabbalism. Christian occultists were attracted

to magical amulets, incantations, demonology, angelology, seals, and letter permutations, using these as basis for ritual magical texts. The Tetragrammaton (YHVH, Yahweh) was held in great awe for power over all things including demons. In late 15th century, Kabbalah was harmonized with Christian doctrines to prove Christ's divinity. Cornelius Agrippa von Nettesheim included Kabbalah in Occult Philosophy (1531). In 16th century, alchemical symbols were integrated into Christian Kabbalah. Renewed attention in 19th century from non-Jewish occultists such as Francis Barrett, Eliphas Levi, and Papus. Levi's works were important in occult revival spreading through Europe; he related Kabbalah to Tarot and numerology and drew connections to Freemasonry, seeing fusion of Judaic kabbalism and Neoplatonic Christianity. He called Qabalah the 'mathematics of human thought,' 'algebra of faith,' solving problems of the soul as equations. The Kabbalah formed central part of teachings of Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn, significant esoteric order in Western mystery tradition flourishing in England late 19th and early 20th centuries. In 1888, founder Samuel Liddle Macgregor Mathers published first English translation of Latin translation of Kabbalah, Kabbala Denuda, by Knorr von Rosenroth, describing Kabbalah as key unlocking mysteries of the Bible. Central concepts: God is Ain Soph (without end, unending), unknowable, unnamable, beyond representation. God created world out of himself but is not diminished; everything remains within him. Aim of humans is to realize union with divine. All things reflected in higher world; nothing exists independently. Humans, by elevating soul to unite with God, elevate all other entities in cosmos. One mystery is why God chose to create imperfect lower worlds—held that he did so to show measure of his goodness. He created world by means of 32 secre

t paths of wisdom, formed of letters and numbers: 22 letters of Hebrew alphabet and 10 sephirot (from Hebrew for sapphire), vessels bearing emanations of God or expressions of God, forming language substituting for God. The sephirot are source from which all numbers emanate and by which all reality is structured. They comprise sacred, unknowable, unspeakable personal name of God: YHVH (Yahweh), the Tetragrammaton. So sacred that other names—Elohim, Adonai, Jehovah—are substituted in Scripture. Letters YHVH correspond to Four Worlds constituting cosmos: Atziluth (world of archetypes and emanation, realm of contemplation, where sephirot exist), Briah/Beriyah (world of creation, archetypal ideas become patterns, Throne of God, realm of meditation), Yetzirah (world of formation, patterns expressed, world of speech, realm of ritual magic), Assiah (world of material, realm of action in daily life). The sephirot form central image of kabbalistic meditation, the Tree of Life, ladder map depicting descent of divine into material world and path by which humans ascend to divine while in flesh. Sephirot channel streams of divine light becoming denser and coarser as they near material plane; divine light flows both down to material world and up to God along these paths. Demonology in Kabbalah: most demon lore is part of Practical Kabbalah, syncretic blend of Talmudic and Midrashic lore with Arabian, Christian, and Eastern European demonologies and folk beliefs. Demons made of fire and air, live in wastelands, associated with cold and north, have subtle bodies allowing flight through air, occupy space between Moon and Earth, have life spans and die but live much longer than humans especially kings and queens. Some such as Lilith and Naamah will live until Judgment Day. Demons often described as under direction of Samael; gather at nocturnal revelries having intercourse with Samael,

similar to Sabbats attended by witches in Christian demonology. Other demons under direction of Ashmedai (Asmodeus), whose name in gematria means 'pharaoh.' Demons linked to left-hand or evil side of sephirot of Tree of Life. Sexual activities between demons and humans prominent; demons cannot reproduce on their own, through sex can multiply and take physical form. Adam spawned hybrid human-demon race continuing through sexual intercourse between humans and demons. Adam and Eve's sons Cain and Abel tainted with impurity of Serpent who slept with Eve and spawned demonic children. Hybrid demon-human children called banim shovavim (mischievous sons); when man dies they attend funeral, lament him, claim inheritance, even injure legitimate sons to get what they want. In 17th century folk customs arose to repel these demons; sometimes legitimate sons forbidden to accompany father's corpse to cemetery; illegitimate demons repelled by circling grave. Demons assigned to all things in creation with angels as counterparts; can be summoned, commanded, repelled in magical rituals according to hours, days, months, planetary aspects, fumes, and seals. Organization of Tree: Each sephirah is state of consciousness and level of attainment in knowledge, mystical steps to unity with God. 10 sephirot arranged in different groups facilitating understanding of meanings. First sephirah Kether (Crown) closest to Ain Soph, source of all life, highest object of prayer. Malkuth (Kingdom) penetrates physical realm, only sephirah in direct contact with it. Lower seven sephirot associated with seven days of creation. Another division splits into two groups of five, upper representing hidden powers, lower five manifest powers. Top three—Kether, Chockmah (Wisdom), Binah (Intelligence)—associated with intellect; middle three—Chesed (Love), Geburah (Strength), Tiphareth (Beauty)—associated with soul;

lower three—Netzach (Victory), Hod (Splendor), Yesod (Foundation)—associated with nature. Each sephirah governed by angels and demons; demonic forces represent chaos and turbulence, used in black magical practices. Sephirot ineffable, descriptions cannot approach true essence; reached only through Chockmah (nonverbal consciousness) and Binah (verbal consciousness), oscillating between states to grasp sephirot. Tree split into three pillars: Right Pillar (masculine, Mercy: Chockmah, Chesed, Netzach), Left Pillar (feminine, Severity: Binah, Geburah, Hod), Middle (Mildness/Moderation: Kether, Tiphareth, Yesod, Malkuth). Middle Pillar alone also called Tree of Knowledge. Sometimes 11th sephirah Daath (Knowledge) included on Middle Pillar below Chockmah and Binah, mediating influences of two, also considered external aspect of Kether; appeared in 13th century, depicted as shadow sphere, cannot be true sephirah as Sefer Yetzirah states only 10 sephirot. Pathways linking sephirot became more complex over time: early 16th century illustrations depict 16 pathways, by 17th century 22 pathways each assigned letter of Hebrew alphabet; God's Creation made through essences of numbers and letters. Sephirot compose unity creating five-dimensional continuum: three dimensions of physical world, plus time, plus spiritual realm. Serve as permanent record of everything ever taken place and ever will—memory of God. Also serve as means of communication with unknowable God; totality expressed in Tetragrammaton. Magical work with Tree: pathways are avenues of navigation on astral plane; communication accomplished through prayer, meditation, contemplation, ritual magic. Traditional meditations of arrays of numbers and Hebrew letters take days to complete. Sephirot contemplated by visualizing them vibrating with colors representing various qualities, together with images of corresponding Hebrew

letters of divine names, planets, angels, metals, parts of body, energy centers; breath and sound utilized to raise consciousness; mantras of arrays of Hebrew letters with specific numerical properties employed.
