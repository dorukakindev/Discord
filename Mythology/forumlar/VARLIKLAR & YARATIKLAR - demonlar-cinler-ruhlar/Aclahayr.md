# Aclahayr
### Genius of Sport
> **Köken:** Nuctemeron (Apollonius of Tyana) · **Kaynak:** Dictionary of Demons, Fred Gettings, 1988; Nuctemeron (Apollonius of Tyana), translated by Eliphas Levi, English edition 1896; De Vita et Morte Moysis, Gilbert Gautrinus, 1721, Book III · **Tür:** demon
*Aclahayr, the genius of sport, is one of the demons of the fourth hour in the NUCTEMERON, a Greek text attributed to Apollonius of Tyana and translated by Eliphas Levi.*

Aclahayr appears in the NUCTEMERON, a Greek text meaning approximately 'night illuminated by day', supposedly written by Apollonius of Tyana and translated into French by Eliphas Levi as a supplement to his Transcendental Magic (English edition 1896). The Greek text was first published by Gilbert Gautrinus in De Vita et Morte Moysis (1721), Book III. The Nuctemeron preserves the names of demons and their supposed rulerships in accordance with the so-called 'twelve symbolical hours, analogous to the signs of the magical Zodiac'. The demons in this system are called 'genii of the hours'. Aclahayr is specifically designated as the demon of the fourth hour and holds the title of 'genius of sport'. The complete list of genii of the hours in the Nuctemeron includes: Aclahayr, Adjuchas, Aeglun, Alphun, Armilus, Azeuph, Baglis, Barcus, Bucaphi, Butatar, Cahor, Camaysar, Causub, Colopatiron, Cuniali, Eirenus (Eirneus), Eirnilus, Eistibus, Haatan, Hahab, Hahabi, Halacho, Hatiphas, Haven, Heiglot, Hizarbin, Jazer, Kalab, Kataris, Labezerin, Labus, Librabis, Marnes, Mascarvin, Mastho, Misran, Mizgitari, Mizkun, Nantur, Nitibus, Nitika, Papus, Phaldor, Phalgus, Pharzuph, Phlogabitus, Rasphuia, Razanil, Risnuch, Rosabis, Sabrus, Sachluph, Sair, Salilus, Schachlil, Schiekron, Sellen, Sezarbil, Sialul, Sinbuck, Sisera, Sislau, Suclagus, Suphlatus, Susabo, Tablibik, Tabris, Tacritan, Tarab, Thagrinus, Toglas, Torvatus, Tukiphat, Zahun, Zalburis, Zaren, Zarobi, Zeffar, Zeirna, Zizuph, Zophas, Zuphlas.
